[Chapter 18](/chapters/ch18-trajectory-planning) fixed the path and optimized the clock. This chapter
lets the path move too, and discovers that once a trajectory is a vector of numbers, "planning"
becomes "descend a cost". Choset gave this two pages of optimal control and three of nonlinear
programming, with the warning quoted above. Twenty years later that warning is the whole discipline:
CHOMP, STOMP, TrajOpt, iLQR and model predictive control are five answers to *which* descent, in
*which* metric, with obstacles as *cost* or as *constraint*.

The idea the chapter unpacks is this. **A trajectory optimizer is a local planner with a global
vocabulary.** It polishes the homotopy class it is handed — brilliantly: smoother, shorter,
dynamically feasible, closed-loop — and it never changes its mind. Hand it the other side of the
obstacle and it will polish that instead. So the pipeline [Chapter 23](/chapters/ch23-capstone) ships
keeps Part III's sampling planners for the global decision and uses this chapter's machinery to make
that decision fast, smooth and safe.

Every method here is *local*, and the prose never says "optimal" without "locally". CHOMP's obstacle
term is a cost, so a collision can survive convergence, and the widget shows it when it happens. Our
`sco` is a recipe in TrajOpt's shape, not TrajOpt's QP-based solver, and says so. iLQR needs a
regularization and a line search, and the defaults are stated. MPC's stability through a terminal
Lyapunov cost is a pointer, not a theorem proved.
</Overview>

## The problem: a jagged path, polished

Take a Chapter 12 RRT path for Reach on the Workbench, seeded, fourteen nodes from the home pose to
the far side of the block. Shortcut it with Chapter 13's greedy shortcutter — three waypoints survive
— resample to twenty-four, and run forty CHOMP steps against the Chapter 7 distance field of the
block. Then hand both the original and the polished path to Chapter 18's time scaler on a horizontal
Workbench with the same motors.

Nothing about the planner's global decision changed: both paths go around the block the same way.
What changed is everything the sampling planner never cared about — curvature, clearance, and
therefore the clock. The question of this chapter is how to make that change deliberately, and what
"deliberately" cannot do.

## Building intuition

### A rubber band in a force field

Think of the discretized trajectory $\xi = (q_1, \dots, q_{N-1})$ as a chain of beads on a rubber
band with its ends pinned at $q_0$ and $q_N$. Smoothness pulls each bead toward the midpoint of its
neighbours. The obstacle term is a force field: zero beyond a margin $\epsilon$ from the nearest
obstacle, growing as a bead approaches, pushing straight out along $\nabla D$. CHOMP is what the
band does when you let go — with one twist that makes it work on robots: a push on one bead is
spread along the whole band by $A^{-1}$, so the band translates and bends instead of growing a kink
where it was touched.

Three things to notice, each of which becomes a theorem below.

**The step size has a stability bound, and it grows with the path's length.** Slide $\eta$ down and
the band oscillates — on the micro-example preset it flips between the disc's centre and the edge of
the margin. The bound is $\eta > \tfrac12(1 + (\lambda/\epsilon)/\lambda_{\min}(A))$: for one free
waypoint and $\epsilon = \tfrac12$ it is $(1 + \lambda)/2$; for twenty waypoints it is about $90$,
because $A^{-1}$ amplifies a low-frequency push by $(m+1)^2/\pi^2$. Above the bound, the step that
is right for a short path crawls on a long one.

**The answer depends on the initial path.** The two initial paths on the Workbench start in
different homotopy classes around the block. CHOMP lowers the cost of both and they end $1.98$ rad
apart at mid-path. From the straight chart line it converges — gradient at machine precision — with
a body point still inside the block, at $D = -0.183$ m, and the badge says **converged in collision**.
That is not a bug in the optimizer. The obstacle term is a cost; the smoothness term is a cost; the
sum has a stationary point there, and a gradient method finds stationary points.

**Clearance is bought with $\lambda$ and $\epsilon$, not asked for.** On the micro-example preset the
single waypoint settles where $2y = \lambda(2 - 2y)$: at $y^* = \lambda/(1 + \lambda)$, clearance
$D^* = (\lambda - 1)/(2(1 + \lambda))$ — zero at $\lambda = 1$, $1/6$ at $2$, $0.3$ at $4$. Raise
$\lambda$ and the band stands further off; it never stands off by exactly what you need. The
sequential-convex recipe later in the chapter is the method that does.

<KeyIdea title="The one-sentence version">
Every optimizer in this chapter minimizes a cost over a finite vector of trajectory parameters by
local steps — first-order in some metric, second-order in a local model, or by sampling — and so
every one of them converges to the nearest stationary point of the basin it started in. What they
differ in is the metric, the model, and whether obstacles are a cost or a constraint.
</KeyIdea>

## The mathematics

### Definitions

**Direct trajectory planning** (Choset §11.3) searches the full state space for $(x(t), u(t))$
jointly, rather than a path first and a clock second.

**An extremal control** satisfies $\partial H/\partial u = 0$ along the trajectory — necessary, not
sufficient; it is **locally optimal** if also $\partial^2 H/\partial u^2 > 0$ (the Legendre–Clebsch
condition, eq. 11.29). A **singular optimal control** is extremal with $\partial^2 H/\partial u^2$
only semidefinite: the min-time double integrator, and Chapter 18's zero-inertia arcs.

**Transcription** replaces $(x(t), u(t))$ by a finite $X$ and the continuous constraints by finitely
many checks at $j\Delta t$ (eqs. 11.40–11.45).

**The covariant gradient** is steepest descent in the metric $A$: $\bar\nabla\mathcal{F} =
A^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{F}$, the direction that most decreases $\mathcal{F}$ per unit of
$\tfrac12\,\delta\xi\T A\,\delta\xi$ — a perturbation measured by how much it changes the *whole*
path's smoothness, not by how far one bead moves.

**Receding horizon (MPC).** Each tick, solve a horizon-$N_h$ problem from the measured state, apply
the first control, discard the rest, repeat.

### Pontryagin and the min-effort double integrator

<KeyIdea title="Statement (Choset Example 11.3.1)">
For $\ddot q = u$, $\ell = u^2$, rest to rest over distance $d$ in fixed $t_f$, the extremal control is
$u(t) = 6d/t_f^2 - 12d\,t/t_f^3$, and it is a local minimum.
</KeyIdea>

<Derivation title="The minimum principle on the simplest system" result="u(t) = 6d/t_f^2 - 12d\,t/t_f^3,\quad \partial^2 H/\partial u^2 = 2 > 0">
**Step 1 — the Hamiltonian.** With $x = (q, \dot q)$, $f = (x_2, u)$ and $\ell = u^2$:
$H = u^2 + \lambda_1 x_2 + \lambda_2 u$ (eq. 11.26).

**Step 2 — extremality.** $\partial H/\partial u = 2u + \lambda_2 = 0$, so $u = -\lambda_2/2$ (eq. 11.31).

**Step 3 — the adjoint equation.** $\dot\lambda = -\partial H/\partial x$ gives $\dot\lambda_1 = 0$ and
$\dot\lambda_2 = -\lambda_1$ (eq. 11.32): $\lambda_1$ is constant, $\lambda_2$ is affine in $t$, and
therefore so is $u(t) = c_0 + c_1 t$.

**Step 4 — the terminal conditions.** $x_2(t_f) = \int_0^{t_f} u\,dt = 0$ gives $c_0 t_f + c_1 t_f^2/2 = 0$;
$x_1(t_f) = \int_0^{t_f}\!\int_0^t u = d$ gives $c_0 t_f^2/2 + c_1 t_f^3/6 = d$. Solving:
$c_0 = 6d/t_f^2$, $c_1 = -12d/t_f^3$.

**Step 5 — convexity.** $\partial^2 H/\partial u^2 = 2 > 0$: the extremal is a local minimum. The
position is the cubic $q(t) = 3d(t/t_f)^2 - 2d(t/t_f)^3$ — the minimum-acceleration-energy profile every
spline library rediscovers.

**Discretized and solved numerically.** With $N = 100$ stages of $\Delta t = 0.01$, $\ell_k = u_k^2\Delta t$
and a terminal weight of $10^6$ on $\|x_N - (1, 0)\|^2$, iLQR's first and only iteration returns
$u_0 = 5.9405$ against Pontryagin's $6 - 12 \cdot 0.005 = 5.94$, with $\max_k |u_k - (6 - 12\,t_{k+1/2})|
= 5.1 \times 10^{-4}$ and $x_N = (1, 0)$ to $10^{-4}$. One iteration, because the problem is
linear-quadratic — the next derivation but one says why.
</Derivation>

### Minimum time is bang-bang, by the minimum principle

<KeyIdea title="Statement (Choset §11.3.1)">
With $\ell = 1$, $|u| \le u_{\max}$ and $t_f$ free, $u^* = -u_{\max}\,\mathrm{sign}(\lambda_2)$, with at most one switch.
</KeyIdea>

<Derivation title="Where the control set's boundary does the work" result="u^* = -u_{\max}\,\mathrm{sign}\,\lambda_2,\ \text{one switch at } t_s = \sqrt{d/u_{\max}}">
**Step 1 — no $u$ in $\partial H/\partial u$.** $H = 1 + \lambda_1 x_2 + \lambda_2 u$ is affine in $u$;
$\partial H/\partial u = \lambda_2$ says nothing about $u$, and $\partial^2 H/\partial u^2 = 0$, so
Legendre–Clebsch is silent. The extremality condition 11.28 is useless here.

**Step 2 — use the inequality instead.** The minimum principle 11.27 says $H^* \le H$ for every
feasible $u$: $\lambda_2 u^* \le \lambda_2 u$ for all $|u| \le u_{\max}$.

**Step 3 — so $u^*$ is at a bound.** If $\lambda_2 < 0$ the inequality forces $u^* = u_{\max}$; if
$\lambda_2 > 0$, $u^* = -u_{\max}$. The control lives on the boundary of its set — bang-bang.

**Step 4 — at most one switch.** $\lambda_2$ is affine in $t$ (same adjoint equation), so it changes
sign at most once. Rest to rest over $d$: accelerate for $t_s$, brake for $t_s$, with $u_{\max}t_s^2 = d$,
so $t_s = \sqrt{d/u_{\max}}$ — the $0.3963$ s of Chapter 18's micro-example read off a Hamiltonian
instead of a phase plane.

**Shooting.** When the extremality conditions cannot be solved by hand, guess $\lambda(0)$, integrate
state and adjoint with $u$ from $\partial H/\partial u = 0$, compare $x(t_f)$ with the target, and
correct the guess — typically by a Gauss–Newton step on the terminal miss. This is where the
"initial guess" problem is first explicit, and `optim::lm` below is the corrector.
</Derivation>

### Transcription

<KeyIdea title="Statement (Choset §11.3.2)">
The program 11.33–11.39 becomes the finite program 11.40–11.45 once (a) $q(t)$, or (b) $u(t)$, or
(c) both are parameterized and the inequality constraints are enforced at $k + 1$ points; (a) computes
$u$ from the dynamics, (b) integrates them, (c) adds the dynamics as equality constraints.
</KeyIdea>

<Derivation title="Three ways to make a trajectory a vector" result="X \in \R^p;\ \min J(X)\ \text{s.t. } g(X) \le 0,\ h(X) = 0">
**Step 1 — choose a basis.** Choset's list: polynomial coefficients, a truncated Fourier series, spline
coefficients, wavelets, piecewise-constant accelerations or forces. The basis decides whether one
parameter moves the whole trajectory or a finite support, which decides the conditioning of
everything downstream.

**Step 2 — sample the constraints.** Torque limits 11.42 and obstacle constraints 11.43 are checked at
$j\Delta t$, $j = 0 \dots k$; endpoint conditions 11.44–11.45 are equalities on $X$.

**Step 3 — hand it to a solver.** Sequential quadratic programming needs $J$, $g$, $h$ and their
gradients, usually a Hessian approximation too, and all of it at least $C^1$ in $X$. Choset's two
warnings are exactly the two the modern methods still trip on: smoothness in $X$, and local optima.

**The modern names.** (a) is *state collocation*: CHOMP's $\xi$ is a sampled $q(t)$, and its
smoothness functional is the acceleration energy the dynamics would charge. (b) is *direct
shooting*: iLQR parameterizes $u_k$ and integrates $f$ to get $x_k$. (c) is *full collocation*:
TrajOpt carries both and enforces the dynamics as equalities. Choset's penalty variant — constraints
folded into the objective — is CHOMP's obstacle term and TrajOpt's outer loop.
</Derivation>

### CHOMP's covariant functional gradient

<KeyIdea title="Statement (Ratliff et al. 2009; Zucker et al. 2013)">
With $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F}_{smooth} + \lambda_{obs}\mathcal{F}_{obs}$, $\nabla\mathcal{F}_{smooth} = A\xi + b$,
and for a body point $x(q, \beta)$ moving at $\|x'\|$ along the path
<LinkedMath>
$$
\nabla_{q}\mathcal{F}_{obs} = \sum_\beta \htmlClass{term-robot}{J_\beta\T}\,\|x'\|\,\Big[(\mathbb{1} - \hat x'\hat x'\T)\,\htmlClass{term-cobstacle}{\nabla c} - c\,\kappa\Big],
\qquad
\xi \leftarrow \xi - \tfrac1\eta\,\htmlClass{term-path}{A^{-1}}\nabla\mathcal{F}.
$$
</LinkedMath>
</KeyIdea>

<Derivation title="Steepest descent in the smoothness metric" result="\xi \leftarrow \xi - \tfrac1\eta A^{-1}(A\xi + b + \lambda\nabla\mathcal{F}_{obs})">
**Step 1 — the smoothness functional is a quadratic.** $\mathcal{F}_{smooth} = \tfrac12\sum_k\|q_{k+1} - q_k\|^2
= \tfrac12\|\mathsf{E}\xi + e\|^2$ with $\mathsf{E}$ the finite-difference operator on the free
waypoints and $e$ carrying the fixed ends. Its gradient is $A\xi + b$ with $A = \mathsf{E}\T\mathsf{E} =
\mathrm{tridiag}(-1, 2, -1) \otimes \mathbb{1}$, i.e. $2q_k - q_{k-1} - q_{k+1}$ at each free waypoint.

**Step 2 — the obstacle functional is an arc-length integral.** $\mathcal{F}_{obs} = \sum_\beta \int_0^1
c(D(x_\beta(q(s))))\,\|x_\beta'\|\,ds$: cost per metre travelled by each body point, so retiming the
path does not change it, and a longer path through cost costs more.

**Step 3 — differentiate under the integral.** The variation of $c(D(x))\|x'\|$ with respect to the
body point's path gives the Euler–Lagrange expression $\|x'\|\big[(\mathbb{1} - \hat x'\hat x'\T)\nabla c - c\,\kappa\big]$,
with $\kappa = (\mathbb{1} - \hat x'\hat x'\T)x''/\|x'\|^2$ the path's curvature. The projection
$(\mathbb{1} - \hat x'\hat x'\T)$ discards the component of $\nabla c$ along the path — pushing a
bead *along* the band does nothing but retime it — and the curvature term says that where the cost is
positive, straightening the path shortens the exposure.

**Step 4 — pull back to joint space.** A workspace force $f$ at body point $\beta$ is the joint
torque $J_\beta\T f$ — Chapter 7's §4.7 lift, Chapter 17's eq. 10.12, the same virtual-work identity
every time. Summing over body points gives $\nabla_q\mathcal{F}_{obs}$ at each waypoint. The library
uses Chapter 7's `controlPointJacobian` for $J_\beta$ unchanged.

**Step 5 — the covariant step.** Minimize the first-order model $\mathcal{F}(\xi) + \nabla\mathcal{F}\T\delta\xi$
subject to $\tfrac12\,\delta\xi\T A\,\delta\xi \le$ const. The Lagrange condition gives
$\delta\xi \propto -A^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{F}$: steepest descent in the metric that measures a
perturbation by the smoothness it costs. $A$ is block-tridiagonal, so $A^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{F}$ is one
Thomas sweep per coordinate, $O(N)$; the library's sweep agrees with the dense inverse to
$4 \times 10^{-15}$.

**The obstacle cost and its slope.** $c(d) = -d + \epsilon/2$ for $d < 0$, $(\epsilon - d)^2/(2\epsilon)$
on $[0, \epsilon]$, $0$ above: $C^1$ at both seams, with $c'(0) = -1$ on both sides. The *pointwise*
form $\nabla_q\mathcal{F}_{obs} = \sum_\beta J_\beta\T c'(D)\nabla D$ drops the arc-length weighting; it
is what the micro-example pins exactly, and the library checks it against finite differences of its
cost to $4 \times 10^{-10}$.

**Why $A^{-1}$ spreads a push.** $A^{-1}$ is dense and positive: its columns are tent functions
peaked at the pushed waypoint and decaying linearly to the pinned ends. A force on one bead moves
every bead, most where it was pushed — the band bends rather than kinks.

**The step bound.** Linearize the obstacle term as a Hessian of at most $\lambda/\epsilon$ per
waypoint ($c'' = 1/\epsilon$ in the margin, $\|\nabla D\| \le 1$). The iteration matrix is
$\mathbb{1} - \tfrac1\eta(\mathbb{1} + A^{-1}H)$ with eigenvalues $1 - (1 + \mu_i)/\eta$,
$\mu_i \in [0, (\lambda/\epsilon)/\lambda_{\min}(A)]$ and $\lambda_{\min}(A) = 2 - 2\cos(\pi/(m+1))$.
It is a contraction iff $\eta > \tfrac12(1 + (\lambda/\epsilon)/\lambda_{\min}(A))$. For $m = 1$,
$\epsilon = \tfrac12$: $\eta > (1 + \lambda)/2$. For $m = 20$, $\lambda = 2$, $\epsilon = \tfrac12$:
$\eta^* = 90.0$; the library runs at $2\eta^*$ without a single cost increase in $400$ steps and at
$\eta^*/4$ records $36$ increases in $100$. The bound is worst-case — a path with few waypoints
inside the margin tolerates a smaller $\eta$ — and it is the mechanism behind the widget's headline.

**STOMP's gradient-free replacement.** Sample $\delta\xi_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, A^{-1})$ for $K$
rollouts — smooth noise, because its covariance is the smoothness metric — evaluate
$S_k = \mathcal{F}(\xi + \delta\xi_k)$, weight $w_k \propto \exp(-h(S_k - S_{\min})/(S_{\max} - S_{\min}))$,
update by $\sum_k w_k\delta\xi_k$ smoothed once more through $A^{-1}$. No gradient is formed, so any
cost that can be *evaluated* can be optimized. On the micro-example at $\lambda = 2$, seed 7, $K = 24$,
120 iterations end at $y = 0.6566$ against CHOMP's $y^* = 0.6667$, with the accepted cost never rising.
</Derivation>

**The micro-example, step by step.** A point robot, three waypoints with the ends fixed: $q_0 = (0,0)$,
$q_2 = (2, 0)$, free $q_1 = (1, 0.2)$; one disc at $(1, 0)$ of radius $\tfrac12$, so
$D(q_1) = 0.2 - 0.5 = -0.3$ (inside) and $\nabla D = (0, 1)$; $\epsilon = \tfrac12$, $\lambda = 1$,
$\eta = 1$, unit spacing, pointwise gradient. Smoothness: $\nabla_{q_1}\mathcal{F}_{smooth} = 2q_1 - q_0 - q_2 = (0, 0.4)$,
$A = 2$. Obstacle: $D < 0$, so $c = 0.55$ and $c' = -1$, giving $\nabla_{q_1}\mathcal{F}_{obs} = (0, -1)$.
Total $(0, -0.6)$; covariant step $q_1 \leftarrow (1, 0.2) - \tfrac11\cdot\tfrac12(0, -0.6) = (1, 0.5)$:
one step lands the waypoint exactly on the disc, $D = 0$. Second step: $\nabla\mathcal{F}_{smooth} = (0, 1.0)$,
$c'(0) = -1$ so $\nabla\mathcal{F}_{obs} = (0, -1)$, total $0$ — a fixed point *in contact*. In general,
inside the margin $c' = -(\epsilon - D)/\epsilon$ and the equilibrium is $y^* = \lambda/(1 + \lambda)$,
clearance $D^* = (\lambda - 1)/(2(1 + \lambda))$: $0$, $1/6$, $0.3$ for $\lambda = 1, 2, 4$, which the
library reaches to $10^{-4}$ in 200 iterations at $\eta = 4$. The lesson: clearance is bought with
$\lambda$ and $\epsilon$; it is never guaranteed.

### The iLQR backward pass

<KeyIdea title="Statement">
With
$Q_x = \ell_x + f_x\T V'_x$, $Q_u = \ell_u + f_u\T V'_x$,
$Q_{xx} = \ell_{xx} + f_x\T V'_{xx} f_x$, $Q_{uu} = \ell_{uu} + f_u\T V'_{xx} f_u$, $Q_{ux} = \ell_{ux} + f_u\T V'_{xx} f_x$,
the optimal local policy is $\delta u = k + \mathbf{K}\,\delta x$ with $k = -Q_{uu}^{-1}Q_u$,
$\mathbf{K} = -Q_{uu}^{-1}Q_{ux}$, and the value model recurses as
$V_x = Q_x - \mathbf{K}\T Q_{uu}k$, $V_{xx} = Q_{xx} - \mathbf{K}\T Q_{uu}\mathbf{K}$.
</KeyIdea>

<Derivation title="Bellman, expanded to second order about a nominal rollout" result="k = -Q_{uu}^{-1}Q_u,\quad \mathbf{K} = -Q_{uu}^{-1}Q_{ux},\quad V_{xx} = Q_{xx} - \mathbf{K}\T Q_{uu}\mathbf{K}">
**Step 1 — Bellman.** $V_k(x) = \min_u\big[\ell(x, u) + V_{k+1}(f(x, u))\big]$, with $V_N = \ell_f$.

**Step 2 — expand about the nominal.** Write $x = \bar x_k + \delta x$, $u = \bar u_k + \delta u$, and
expand the bracket to second order: the coefficients are the $Q$ terms of the statement. Dropping the
second derivatives of $f$ — the tensor terms $V'_x\cdot f_{xx}$, $V'_x\cdot f_{uu}$, $V'_x\cdot f_{ux}$
— is iLQR; keeping them is DDP. The library computes them by central differences of the Jacobians
when `ddp: true`.

**Step 3 — minimize the quadratic in $\delta u$.** $\partial/\partial\delta u = 0$ gives
$Q_u + Q_{uu}\delta u + Q_{ux}\delta x = 0$, i.e. $\delta u = -Q_{uu}^{-1}(Q_u + Q_{ux}\delta x) = k + \mathbf{K}\delta x$.
This is a *policy*: a feedforward correction plus a linear feedback on the deviation from the nominal.

**Step 4 — substitute back.** The minimized quadratic is the new value model:
$V_x = Q_x + \mathbf{K}\T Q_{uu}k + \mathbf{K}\T Q_u + Q_{ux}\T k$ and $V_{xx} = Q_{xx} + \mathbf{K}\T Q_{uu}\mathbf{K} + \mathbf{K}\T Q_{ux} + Q_{ux}\T\mathbf{K}$,
which collapse to the statement's forms once $k$ and $\mathbf{K}$ are substituted (Tassa et al. keep
the cross terms for numerical symmetry; so does the library).

**Step 5 — recurse, then roll out.** From $V_N = \ell_f$ down to $k = 0$. The forward pass is
closed-loop: $u_k = \bar u_k + \alpha k_k + \mathbf{K}_k(x_k - \bar x_k)$, with backtracking on $\alpha$
against the expected decrease $\Delta V(\alpha) = \alpha\sum_k k_k\T Q_u + \tfrac12\alpha^2\sum_k k_k\T Q_{uu}k_k$.
A step is accepted when the realized decrease is a reasonable fraction of the promise.

**Regularization.** $Q_{uu}$ need not be positive definite far from a minimum; the library adds
$\mu\mathbb{1}$, raises $\mu$ tenfold when a Cholesky pivot fails or no $\alpha$ descends, and lowers it
on success. Defaults: $\mu_0 = 10^{-6}$ (zero for linear-quadratic checks), $\alpha \in \{1, \tfrac12, \dots, 1/64\}$.

**On a linear-quadratic problem one pass is Riccati.** If $f = Ax + Bu$ and $\ell$ is quadratic
about the origin, $f_x = A$, $f_u = B$ and the recursion for $V_{xx}$ is exactly the discrete Riccati
recursion $P_k = Q + A\T P_{k+1}A + A\T P_{k+1}B\,\mathbf{K}_k$ with $\mathbf{K}_k = -(R + B\T P_{k+1}B)^{-1}B\T P_{k+1}A$.
The library computes that recursion independently; on a 40-stage double-integrator problem the gains
agree to $1.1 \times 10^{-15}$ and the $\alpha = 1$ forward pass lands on the optimal cost
$\tfrac12 x_0\T P_0 x_0 = 10.88611263$ in one iteration.

**The first nonlinear term that breaks this.** For the unicycle, $f_x$ depends on $\theta$ through
$\cos\theta$, $\sin\theta$; the rollout is no longer the linear prediction, $\alpha = 1$ may overshoot,
and the pass has to be repeated. Hitch's parking problem below takes 79 iLQR iterations, or 30 with
DDP's second-order terms, and the two reach *different* local optima — DDP's cheaper on this instance,
$56.24$ against $60.62$, both parking within 5 cm and 0.05 rad.
</Derivation>

### TrajOpt's sequential convex optimization

<KeyIdea title="Statement (Schulman et al. 2013, 2014)">
Minimize $J(X) + \mu_{pen}\sum_i|g_i(X)|^+$ by repeatedly convexifying $J$ and $g$ about the current
$X$, solving the convex subproblem inside a trust region $\|\delta X\|_\infty \le \Delta$, accepting if
the true merit improves, and increasing $\mu_{pen}$ in an outer loop until the constraints hold.
Collision constraints are $sd(R(q), \WO_i) \ge d_{safe}$, linearized at the witness point as
$sd(q) + \hat n\T J_p(q)\,\delta q$.
</KeyIdea>

<Derivation title="An exact penalty, convexified" result="\text{violation} = 0 \text{ at convergence (for finite } \mu_{pen})">
**Step 1 — the $\ell_1$ penalty is exact.** For a finite $\mu_{pen}$ larger than the constraints'
multipliers, the minimizer of $J + \mu_{pen}\sum|g_i|^+$ satisfies $g_i \le 0$ exactly — not
approximately, as a quadratic penalty would. The hinge is non-smooth, which is why the subproblem is
solved as a convex program rather than by a gradient step.

**Step 2 — convexify.** Replace $J$ by its quadratic model and each $g_i$ by its linearization; the
hinge of an affine function is convex. For collision, $g = d_{safe} - sd(q)$ and the linearization
at the witness pair is $d_{safe} - sd(q_0) - \hat n\T J_p(q_0)\,\delta q$ — exactly the contact normal
times the body-point Jacobian, Chapter 7's lift once more.

**Step 3 — the trust region guarantees descent on the merit.** The convex model agrees with the
true merit to first order; inside a small enough box the true merit decreases when the model's does.
Accept and expand $\Delta$ on success; reject and shrink on failure.

**Step 4 — the penalty loop.** When the inner loop converges with violations left, multiply
$\mu_{pen}$ and repeat. Swept volumes between consecutive footprints are handled as convex hulls of
the two shapes — a recipe this book states and does not implement.

**What our `sco` simplifies, and says so.** The convex subproblem is a QP in TrajOpt. Here it is the
book's own Levenberg–Marquardt on the residual vector $[\mathsf{E}\xi + e;\ \sqrt{\mu}\,|g_{lin}|^+]$,
boxed by the same $\Delta$ — a recipe in TrajOpt's shape, not TrajOpt. On the twelve-waypoint disc
problem it ends after 27 convexifications at $\mu_{pen} = 10^5$ with every waypoint at least
$d_{safe} = 0.1$ from the disc, the tightest at $0.1000$ and a violation of $6 \times 10^{-7}$; CHOMP
on the same scene with $\lambda = 1$, $\epsilon = 0.5$ settles at a clearance of $0.471$ — far more
than asked, because it never saw the constraint. Cost versus constraint, in one number each.
</Derivation>

### MPC: the loop around any of them

<KeyIdea title="Statement">
Each tick: measure $x$; solve the horizon-$N_h$ problem from $x$ with the obstacles where the sensor
says they are; apply $u_0$; shift the solution one stage as the next warm start. The horizon trades
compute for foresight, and past the point where the plan is corrected anyway it buys compute only.
A terminal cost that approximates the cost-to-go is what lets a short horizon see around a corner.
</KeyIdea>

before replanning, collides for 10 ticks where MPC waits. Horizon is not what cured myopia; the
terminal cost was. Stability of the closed loop via a terminal cost that is a Lyapunov function is
the standard theorem (Rawlings, Mayne and Diehl); this chapter points at it and does not prove it.

## The algorithm

<Algorithm
  name="chomp_step(ξ, q₀, q_N, D, {J_β}, ε, λ, η)"
  inputs="free waypoints, fixed ends, distance field with gradient, body-point Jacobians, margin, weight, step"
  outputs="ξ updated in place; F_smooth, F_obs, min clearance"
  complexity="O(N·β) distance queries + one O(N) Thomas sweep per coordinate"
>
1. $g_k \leftarrow 2q_k - q_{k-1} - q_{k+1}$ for each free waypoint (the smoothness gradient $A\xi + b$)
2. for each waypoint $k$ and body point $\beta$: $x \leftarrow x_\beta(q_k)$, $d \leftarrow D(x)$, $f \leftarrow c'(d)\,\nabla D(x)$; in the arclength form, $f \leftarrow \|x'\|\,[(\mathbb{1} - \hat x'\hat x'\T)f - c(d)\,\kappa]$ with $x', x''$ by central differences along $\xi$
3. $g_k \leftarrow g_k + \lambda\,J_\beta(q_k)\T f$
4. solve $A\,\delta = g$ by the Thomas sweep on $\mathrm{tridiag}(-1, 2, -1)$, one coordinate at a time
5. $\xi \leftarrow \xi - \delta/\eta$; report $F_{smooth}$, $F_{obs}$, $\min d$. Stable iff $\eta > \tfrac12(1 + (\lambda/\epsilon)/\lambda_{\min}(A))$.
</Algorithm>

<Algorithm
  name="stomp_step(ξ, K, rng, noise, h)"
  inputs="the same problem, a seeded generator, rollout count, noise scale, temperature"
  outputs="ξ updated if the cost did not rise"
  complexity="K full cost evaluations; no gradients"
>
1. $L L\T = A^{-1}$ once; for $k = 1 \dots K$: $\delta\xi_k \leftarrow \text{noise}\cdot L\,z_k$, $z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbb{1})$ per coordinate
2. $S_k \leftarrow \mathcal{F}(\xi + \delta\xi_k)$; $w_k \leftarrow \exp(-h(S_k - S_{\min})/(S_{\max} - S_{\min}))$, normalized
3. $\delta\xi \leftarrow M\sum_k w_k\delta\xi_k$ with $M = A^{-1}$ column-scaled so its largest entry is $1/m$
4. if $\mathcal{F}(\xi + \delta\xi) \le \mathcal{F}(\xi)$: $\xi \leftarrow \xi + \delta\xi$
</Algorithm>

<Algorithm
  name="sco(ξ₀, sd, d_safe, μ₀, Δ₀)  —  a recipe in TrajOpt's shape"
  inputs="initial waypoints, signed-distance field, clearance, initial penalty and trust radius"
  outputs="ξ with violation below tolerance, the merit history"
  complexity="outer penalty loop × inner convexify–solve–accept loop; each solve a few LM iterations"
>
1. repeat (penalty loop): repeat (inner loop):
2. convexify: at each waypoint record $sd(q_k)$ and $\hat n_k = \nabla sd(q_k)$; model $g_k(\delta) = d_{safe} - sd(q_k) - \hat n_k\T\delta q_k$
3. solve $\min_\delta \tfrac12\|\mathsf{E}(\xi + \delta) + e\|^2 + \mu\sum_k|g_k(\delta)|^+$ with $\|\delta\|_\infty \le \Delta$ — here by LM on the residuals $[\mathsf{E}(\xi + \delta) + e;\ \sqrt\mu\,|g_k(\delta)|^+]$ with the box as trust region (TrajOpt: a QP)
4. if the *true* merit $\tfrac12\|\mathsf{E}\xi + e\|^2 + \mu\sum_k|d_{safe} - sd(q_k)|^+$ decreased: accept, $\Delta \leftarrow 1.5\Delta$; else $\Delta \leftarrow \Delta/2$
5. until four rejections; if the worst violation $\ge$ tol: $\mu \leftarrow 10\mu$ and repeat the penalty loop
</Algorithm>

<Algorithm
  name="ilqr(f, ℓ, ℓ_f, x₀, ū, μ₀)"
  inputs="discrete dynamics with Jacobians, running and terminal cost with derivatives, start, initial controls, regularization"
  outputs="(x̄, ū, {k_k}, {𝐊_k}), the cost per iteration"
  complexity="O(N (n_x + n_u)³) per iteration"
>
1. roll $\bar u$ out from $x_0$; $J \leftarrow \sum\ell + \ell_f$
2. **backward pass:** $V_x, V_{xx} \leftarrow \nabla\ell_f, \nabla^2\ell_f$; for $k = N-1 \dots 0$: form $Q_x, Q_u, Q_{xx}, Q_{uu}, Q_{ux}$ (add $V_x\cdot f_{xx}$ etc. for DDP); if $Q_{uu} + \mu\mathbb{1}$ is not positive definite, $\mu \leftarrow 10\mu$ and restart; $k_k \leftarrow -(Q_{uu} + \mu\mathbb{1})^{-1}Q_u$, $\mathbf{K}_k \leftarrow -(Q_{uu} + \mu\mathbb{1})^{-1}Q_{ux}$; update $V_x, V_{xx}$; accumulate $\Delta V = \alpha a + \alpha^2 b$
3. **forward pass:** for $\alpha \in \{1, \tfrac12, \dots\}$: $u_k \leftarrow \bar u_k + \alpha k_k + \mathbf{K}_k(x_k - \bar x_k)$, $x_{k+1} \leftarrow f(x_k, u_k)$; accept the first $\alpha$ whose realized decrease is a fair fraction of $-\Delta V(\alpha)$; $\mu \leftarrow \mu/10$
4. if no $\alpha$ descends: $\mu \leftarrow 10\mu$; repeat from 2 until the relative decrease is below tol
</Algorithm>

<Algorithm
  name="mpc_tick(x_meas, t)"
  inputs="measured state, tick; a horizon N_h, a cost builder seeing the obstacles at t, a warm start"
  outputs="u₀ to apply; the plan"
  complexity="one warm-started iLQR solve (a few iterations) per tick"
>
1. $\bar u \leftarrow$ last plan shifted by one stage (its last control repeated), or zeros
2. $(\bar x, \bar u) \leftarrow$ `ilqr`$(f, \ell_t, \ell_f, x_{meas}, \bar u)$ with the obstacle frozen where it is at $t$ and $\ell_f$ the cost-to-go when one is available
3. apply $\bar u_0$; keep $\bar u$ for the next tick
</Algorithm>

<Algorithm
  name="optim::lm(r, J_r, x₀, Δ)  —  shared with Chapter 21"
  inputs="residual function (and Jacobian, else central differences), start, optional box trust radius"
  outputs="x minimizing ½‖r(x)‖², cost history"
  complexity="one m×n Jacobian and one n×n solve per trial step"

1. $\lambda \leftarrow 10^{-3}$; repeat:
2. $J \leftarrow J_r(x)$; stop if $\|J\T r\|_\infty <$ tol
3. solve $(J\T J + \lambda\,\mathrm{diag}(J\T J))\,\delta = -J\T r$; clip $\|\delta\|_\infty \le \Delta$
4. if $\tfrac12\|r(x + \delta)\|^2 < \tfrac12\|r(x)\|^2$: accept, $\lambda \leftarrow 0.3\lambda$; else $\lambda \leftarrow 10\lambda$ and retry
</Algorithm>

```rust title="crates/trajopt/src/optim.rs"
use nalgebra::{DMatrix, DVector};

/// A nonlinear least-squares problem ½‖r(x)‖². `jacobian` defaults to central differences.
pub trait LeastSquares {
    fn residual(&self, x: &DVector<f64>) -> DVector<f64>;
    fn jacobian(&self, x: &DVector<f64>) -> DMatrix<f64> { finite_difference_jacobian(|y| self.residual(y), x, 1e-6) }
}

pub struct LmOptions { pub max_iter: usize, pub tol_grad: f64, pub lambda0: f64, pub trust: f64 }

/// Levenberg–Marquardt with Marquardt scaling and a box trust region:
///     (JᵀJ + λ diag(JᵀJ)) δ = −Jᵀr,   ‖δ‖∞ ≤ trust,   accept iff the true cost drops.
/// λ → 0 is Gauss–Newton, λ → ∞ a short gradient step. The box is the crude trust
/// region `sco` leans on, and Chapter 21's nonlinear steering reuses the whole thing.
pub fn levenberg_marquardt(p: &impl LeastSquares, x0: DVector<f64>, o: &LmOptions) -> LmResult {
    let (mut x, mut lambda) = (x0, o.lambda0);
    let mut r = p.residual(&x);
    let mut cost = 0.5 * r.norm_squared();
    for _ in 0..o.max_iter {
        let j = p.jacobian(&x);
        let g = j.transpose() * &r;
        if g.amax() < o.tol_grad { break; }
        let jtj = j.transpose() * &j;
        loop {
            let mut a = jtj.clone();
            for i in 0..a.nrows() { a[(i, i)] += lambda * jtj[(i, i)].max(1e-12); }
            let mut delta = a.lu().solve(&(-&g)).expect("JᵀJ + λD is positive definite");
            let step = delta.amax();
            if step > o.trust { delta *= o.trust / step; }
            let x_trial = &x + &delta;
            let r_trial = p.residual(&x_trial);
            let c_trial = 0.5 * r_trial.norm_squared();
            if c_trial < cost { x = x_trial; r = r_trial; cost = c_trial; lambda *= 0.3; break; }
            lambda *= 10.0;
            if lambda > 1e15 { return LmResult::stationary(x, cost); }
        }
    }
    LmResult::converged(x, cost)
}
```

```rust title="crates/trajopt/src/chomp.rs"
use nalgebra::{Point2, SMatrix, SVector};

/// A discretized trajectory with fixed endpoints; ξ holds only the N−1 free waypoints.
pub struct ChompProblem<'a, const N: usize> {
    pub q0: SVector<f64, N>, pub q_end: SVector<f64, N>,
    pub field: &'a dyn DistanceField,            // Chapter 7: dist(x), grad(x), signed
    /// Body points: workspace position and 2×N Jacobian at q (Reach: samples along both links).
    pub body: Vec<Box<dyn Fn(&SVector<f64, N>) -> (Point2<f64>, SMatrix<f64, 2, N>) + 'a>>,
    pub eps: f64, pub lambda_obs: f64, pub eta: f64,
    pub obstacle_form: ObstacleGradient,        // Pointwise (the micro-example) | ArcLength (full CHOMP)
}

/// c(d): −d + ε/2 below zero, (ε − d)²/(2ε) on [0, ε], 0 above — C¹ at both seams.
pub fn obstacle_cost(d: f64, eps: f64) -> f64 { if d < 0.0 { -d + eps / 2.0 } else if d < eps { (eps - d).powi(2) / (2.0 * eps) } else { 0.0 } }
pub fn obstacle_slope(d: f64, eps: f64) -> f64 { if d < 0.0 { -1.0 } else if d < eps { -(eps - d) / eps } else { 0.0 } }

/// Solve A y = r for A = tridiag(−1, 2, −1) ⊗ I by one Thomas sweep per coordinate: the
/// covariant preconditioner. A⁻¹ is dense and positive — a push on one waypoint moves them all.
pub fn solve_smoothness_metric<const N: usize>(r: &[SVector<f64, N>]) -> Vec<SVector<f64, N>> {
    let m = r.len();
    let mut bp = vec![2.0; m];
    let mut dp: Vec<SVector<f64, N>> = r.to_vec();
    for k in 1..m { let w = -1.0 / bp[k - 1]; bp[k] = 2.0 + w; dp[k] = dp[k] - w * dp[k - 1]; }
    let mut y = vec![SVector::zeros(); m];
    y[m - 1] = dp[m - 1] / bp[m - 1];
    for k in (0..m - 1).rev() { y[k] = (dp[k] + y[k + 1]) / bp[k]; }
    y
}

/// One covariant step ξ ← ξ − (1/η) A⁻¹ ∇F. Returns the statistics *before* the step.
pub fn chomp_step<const N: usize>(p: &ChompProblem<'_, N>, xi: &mut [SVector<f64, N>]) -> ChompStats {
    let full: Vec<_> = std::iter::once(p.q0).chain(xi.iter().copied()).chain(std::iter::once(p.q_end)).collect();
    let mut grad: Vec<SVector<f64, N>> = (1..full.len() - 1).map(|k| 2.0 * full[k] - full[k - 1] - full[k + 1]).collect();
    let (mut f_obs, mut min_clear) = (0.0, f64::INFINITY);
    for k in 1..full.len() - 1 {
        for b in &p.body {
            let (x, j) = b(&full[k]);
            let d = p.field.dist(&x);
            min_clear = min_clear.min(d);
            let c = obstacle_cost(d, p.eps);
            let mut f = obstacle_slope(d, p.eps) * p.field.grad(&x);          // ∇c = c′(D) ∇D
            let mut w = 1.0;
            if p.obstacle_form == ObstacleGradient::ArcLength {
                let (xm, _) = b(&full[k - 1]); let (xp, _) = b(&full[k + 1]);
                let v = 0.5 * (xp - xm); let acc = xp - 2.0 * x + xm;        // x′, x″ along the path
                if let Some(t) = v.try_normalize(1e-9) {
                    let proj = |z: nalgebra::Vector2<f64>| z - t * t.dot(&z);
                    let kappa = proj(acc.coords) / v.norm_squared();
                    f = v.norm() * (proj(f) - c * kappa);                   // ‖x′‖[(I − x̂′x̂′ᵀ)∇c − c κ]
                    w = v.norm();
                }
            }
            f_obs += c * w;
            grad[k - 1] += p.lambda_obs * (j.transpose() * f);               // Jᵀ f: Chapter 7's lift
        }
    }
    let dir = solve_smoothness_metric(&grad);
    for (q, d) in xi.iter_mut().zip(dir) { *q -= d / p.eta; }
    ChompStats { f_smooth: smoothness(&full), f_obs, min_clearance: min_clear }
}

/// Sufficient step bound: η > ½(1 + (λ/ε)/λ_min(A)), λ_min(A) = 2 − 2cos(π/(m+1)).
pub fn stable_eta(m: usize, lambda_obs: f64, eps: f64) -> f64 {
    0.5 * (1.0 + lambda_obs / eps / (2.0 - 2.0 * (std::f64::consts::PI / (m as f64 + 1.0)).cos()))
}
```

```rust title="crates/trajopt/src/ilqr.rs"
/// Discrete-time dynamics x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k) with Jacobians; implemented by `models::*`.
pub trait DiscreteDynamics<const NX: usize, const NU: usize> {
    fn step(&self, x: &SVector<f64, NX>, u: &SVector<f64, NU>) -> SVector<f64, NX>;
    /// (f_x, f_u). Default: central differences — override with analytic forms for speed.
    fn jacobians(&self, x: &SVector<f64, NX>, u: &SVector<f64, NU>) -> (SMatrix<f64, NX, NX>, SMatrix<f64, NX, NU>) {
        numeric_jacobians(|a, b| self.step(a, b), x, u, 1e-6)
    }
}

pub struct BackwardPass<const NX: usize, const NU: usize> {
    pub k: Vec<SVector<f64, NU>>,          // feedforward
    pub big_k: Vec<SMatrix<f64, NU, NX>>,  // feedback gains 𝐊_k — the policy, not just a plan
    pub v_xx: Vec<SMatrix<f64, NX, NX>>,   // the value model, for the widget's ellipses
    pub expected_decrease: (f64, f64),     // ΔV(α) = α·a + α²·b, for the line search
    pub ok: bool,                          // every regularized Q_uu was positive definite
}

pub fn backward_pass<const NX: usize, const NU: usize>(
    f: &impl DiscreteDynamics<NX, NU>, cost: &impl StageCost<NX, NU>,
    xs: &[SVector<f64, NX>], us: &[SVector<f64, NU>], mu: f64, ddp: bool,
) -> BackwardPass<NX, NU> {
    let n = us.len();
    let (mut v_x, mut v_xx) = cost.terminal_derivs(&xs[n]);
    let mut bp = BackwardPass::with_capacity(n);
    let (mut a, mut b) = (0.0, 0.0);
    for k in (0..n).rev() {
        let (fx, fu) = f.jacobians(&xs[k], &us[k]);
        let l = cost.running_derivs(&xs[k], &us[k], k);
        let q_x  = l.lx + fx.transpose() * v_x;
        let q_u  = l.lu + fu.transpose() * v_x;
        let mut q_xx = l.lxx + fx.transpose() * v_xx * fx;
        let mut q_uu = l.luu + fu.transpose() * v_xx * fu;
        let mut q_ux = l.lux + fu.transpose() * v_xx * fx;
        if ddp {                                   // the tensor terms V_x · f_xx etc., by differencing the Jacobians
            let so = second_order_terms(f, &xs[k], &us[k], &v_x);
            q_xx += so.xx; q_uu += so.uu; q_ux += so.ux;
        }
        let q_uu_reg = q_uu + mu * SMatrix::identity();
        let Some(chol) = q_uu_reg.cholesky() else { bp.ok = false; return bp; };
        let kk = -chol.solve(&q_u);
        let big_k = -chol.solve(&q_ux);
        a += kk.dot(&q_u);
        b += 0.5 * kk.dot(&(q_uu * kk));
        // Value update with the cross terms (Tassa, Erez & Todorov 2012, eq. 11).
        v_x  = q_x + big_k.transpose() * q_uu * kk + big_k.transpose() * q_u + q_ux.transpose() * kk;
        v_xx = q_xx + big_k.transpose() * q_uu * big_k + big_k.transpose() * q_ux + q_ux.transpose() * big_k;
        v_xx = 0.5 * (v_xx + v_xx.transpose());
        bp.push(k, kk, big_k, v_xx);
    }
    bp.expected_decrease = (a, b);
    bp
}

/// Closed-loop rollout u_k = ū_k + α k_k + 𝐊_k (x_k − x̄_k): the policy, not the plan.
pub fn forward_pass<const NX: usize, const NU: usize>(f: &impl DiscreteDynamics<NX, NU>, xs: &[SVector<f64, NX>], us: &[SVector<f64, NU>], bp: &BackwardPass<NX, NU>, alpha: f64)
    -> (Vec<SVector<f64, NX>>, Vec<SVector<f64, NU>>) {
    let mut xs_new = vec![xs[0]];
    let mut us_new = Vec::with_capacity(us.len());
    for k in 0..us.len() {
        let u = us[k] + alpha * bp.k[k] + bp.big_k[k] * (xs_new[k] - xs[k]);
        xs_new.push(f.step(&xs_new[k], &u));
        us_new.push(u);
    }
    (xs_new, us_new)
}

pub fn ilqr<const NX: usize, const NU: usize>(f: &impl DiscreteDynamics<NX, NU>, cost: &impl StageCost<NX, NU>, x0: SVector<f64, NX>, u_init: &[SVector<f64, NU>], opts: IlqrOptions) -> IlqrResult<NX, NU> {
    let mut us = u_init.to_vec();
    let mut xs = rollout(f, x0, &us);
    let mut j = total_cost(cost, &xs, &us);
    let mut mu = opts.mu0;
    for _ in 0..opts.max_iter {
        let mut bp = backward_pass(f, cost, &xs, &us, mu, opts.ddp);
        while !bp.ok { mu = (mu * 10.0).max(1e-6); bp = backward_pass(f, cost, &xs, &us, mu, opts.ddp); }
        let mut accepted = false;
        for &alpha in &[1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625, 0.03125, 0.015625] {
            let (xs_t, us_t) = forward_pass(f, &xs, &us, &bp, alpha);
            let j_t = total_cost(cost, &xs_t, &us_t);
            let expected = -(alpha * bp.expected_decrease.0 + alpha * alpha * bp.expected_decrease.1);
            if j_t < j && (expected <= 1e-12 || (j - j_t) / expected > 1e-4) {
                let rel = (j - j_t) / j.abs().max(1e-300);
                xs = xs_t; us = us_t; j = j_t; mu = (mu * 0.1).max(opts.mu_min); accepted = true;
                if rel < opts.tol { return IlqrResult::converged(xs, us, bp); }
                break;
            }
        }
        if !accepted { mu *= 10.0; if mu > opts.mu_max { break; } }
    }
    IlqrResult::stopped(xs, us)
}
```

```rust title="crates/trajopt/src/mpc.rs"
/// Receding horizon around any solver that accepts a warm start.
pub struct Mpc<S: HorizonSolver> { pub solver: S, pub horizon: usize, warm: Option<Vec<S::Control>> }

impl<S: HorizonSolver> Mpc<S> {
    /// One tick: solve from the *measured* state with the obstacles where the sensor says they
    /// are, apply the first control, keep the rest shifted by one stage as the next warm start.
    pub fn tick(&mut self, x_meas: &S::State, t: usize) -> S::Control {
        let init = match &self.warm {
            Some(w) => { let mut u = w[1..].to_vec(); u.push(*w.last().unwrap()); u }
            None => vec![S::Control::zeros(); self.horizon],
        };
        let plan = self.solver.solve(x_meas, &init, t);        // iLQR, a few iterations
        let u0 = plan.controls[0];
        self.warm = Some(plan.controls);
        u0
    }
}

/// Chapter 6's wave-front distance to the goal on an occupancy grid, bilinearly interpolated:
/// the terminal cost that lets a short horizon see around a corner.
pub struct GridCostToGo { grid: OccupancyGrid, value: Vec<f64> }
impl GridCostToGo {
    pub fn new(world: &World, goal: Point2<f64>, cell: f64, inflation: f64) -> Self {
        let grid = grid_from_world(world, cell, inflation);
        let wf = wavefront(&grid, grid.cell_at(goal), Connectivity::Eight);   // Chapter 7's wave-front on Chapter 6's grid
        let value = wf.label.iter().map(|&l| if l >= 2 { (l - 2) as f64 * cell } else { f64::INFINITY }).collect();
        Self { grid, value }
    }
    pub fn at(&self, x: f64, y: f64) -> f64 { self.grid.bilinear(&self.value, x, y) }
}
```

## Putting it together

The Reach pipeline the capstone ships is the hook: a Chapter 12 RRT → Chapter 13 shortcut → `chomp`
→ Chapter 18 `CubicSpline` + `time_scale` → Chapter 17 replay. On the seeded instance above it takes
the time-optimal execution from $1.803$ s to $0.992$ s without changing the planner's global decision.
Three honesty items the pipeline makes visible.

**Where optimization beats search.** Smoothness, clearance and dynamics are what a sampling planner
ignores and an optimizer measures. Forty covariant steps cost less than the RRT that found the path,
and the time scaler rewards every bit of curvature removed. iLQR goes further than CHOMP can: it
respects the dynamics by construction, and it returns a feedback policy — the gains $\mathbf{K}_k$ —
which is what makes the receding-horizon loop closed-loop rather than a replanner.

**Where it sticks.** Homotopy and narrow passages. From the straight chart line, CHOMP on the
Workbench converges with a body point inside the block, at machine-precision stationarity. No
$\eta$, $\lambda$ or $\epsilon$ fixes that; only a different initial path does, which is a sampling
planner's job. Choset's "the solution achieved will depend heavily on the initial guess" is the
chapter's epigraph for a reason.

**Where the loop earns its keep.** MPC's value is not foresight — a frozen-obstacle planner has
none past its sensor — but correction: the obstacle moves, the plan moves with it, every tick. The
Apartment check shows the horizon buying compute ($1.9 \to 10.0$ ms) while the terminal cost-to-go,
not the horizon, decides whether Rusty arrives at all. The sister book's MPPI runs the same loop
with sampling in place of iLQR; a side-by-side on the same model, horizon and seeds is the honest way
to compare them, and anything less is not shown.
